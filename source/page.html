Reading guide

Word rows

Each language is a row, read left to right in its own word order. Lines link each word to its English meaning and to the matching word in the next row: Taishanese, Cantonese, English, Mandarin, then Spanish, French and German. Tap a word to trace it.

A dashed box is a particle with no English equivalent. A ? marks a word that couldn't be confirmed. English in (parentheses) is implied or describes a tone.

The round button at the start of each row plays the whole line in that language. Each word box shows two spellings: the standard romanization on top (Jyutping, pinyin or Stephen Li's Taishanese, with tone numbers), and an easy English sound-alike below in italics. Notes use easy English by default; switch them to the standard systems under View.

Translations of new sentences are word-by-word builds: they keep English word order and skip most particles, so treat them as a starting point.

Listen uses your device's own Cantonese, Mandarin, Spanish, French and German voices. No phone ships a Taishanese voice, so this app has its own Taishanese speech engine. It builds every consonant, vowel and tone from the romanization, including the hissy ɬ and the rising changed tones. It sounds robotic, but it works even with the phone on silent. Tapping a word also speaks it.

Spanish follows Latin American usage (ustedes, manejar, la alberca). Lines use the friendly tú, tu and du forms you would use with family.

Cantonese tones

Each line is your voice's pitch, low at the bottom and high at the top, ending at the dot. Same sound si, six meanings. Tone 1 sounds like Mandarin's 1st tone (ā), tone 2 like Mandarin's 2nd (á).

Taishanese tones

Five main tones, shown on hau. The digits are pitch levels from 1 (low) to 5 (high).

ɬa hissy "thl", like Welsh "ll"; replaces Cantonese s ŋ"ng" as in sing, even at the start of a word ɔ"aw" as in law ə"uh" as in sofa ɛ"e" as in bet

Mandarin tones

Pinyin marks the tone: ā high flat, á rising, ǎ dip, à falling, a light.
