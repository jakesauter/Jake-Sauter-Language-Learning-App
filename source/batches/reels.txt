@ Word Fun | Forget it.
g: Forget it
y: 算啦/syun3 laa1/0
m: 算了/suàn le/0
t: 罷就/ba32 dziau32/0
s: Olvídalo/0
f: Laisse tomber/0
d: Vergiss es/0
n: 算 means "count": 算了 is "count it as done", so let it go. Cantonese says 算啦 syun3 laa1 (or 罷就 baa6 zau6).
nt: Stephen Li's dictionary: 罷就 ba32 dziau32 "drop it, let it go". Avoid the look-alike with 𠹌, it's vulgar.

@ Word Fun | A stir (a sound or movement)
g: move / still
y: 動/dung6/0 ; 靜/zing6/1
m: 动/dòng/0 ; 静/jing/1
t: 動/əŋ32/0 ; 靜/dein32/1
s: Ruido/0,1
f: Bruit/0,1
d: Geräusch/0,1
n: Opposites make a new word: 動 "move" + 靜 "still" = 動靜, "any sign of activity", as in 有冇動靜? "Anything happening?" In Mandarin the second syllable goes toneless: dòngjing.
nt: 動 əŋ32 (from 運動 vun32 əŋ32) and 靜 dein32 are both in Stephen Li's dictionary. Not confirmed as one word in Taishanese, so check with family.

@ Word Fun | Thing, stuff
g: east / west
y: 東/dung1/0 ; 西/sai1/1
m: 东/dōng/0 ; 西/xi/1
t: 東/əŋ33/0 ; 西/ɬai33/1
s: Cosa/0,1
f: Truc/0,1
d: Ding/0,1
n: 東 "east" + 西 "west" = 東西, "a thing". A popular story: old Chang'an had an East Market and a West Market, so "buying east-west" meant going shopping. In everyday Cantonese the word for "thing" is 嘢 je5, and 東西 sounds bookish.
nt: Stephen Li's dictionary: 東 əŋ33, 西 ɬai33. Everyday Taishanese says 嘢 yia33 for "thing".

@ Word Fun | Business, trade
g: buy / sell
y: 買/maai5/0 ; 賣/maai6/1
m: 买/mǎi/0 ; 卖/mai/1
t: 買/mai33/0 ; 賣/mai32/1
s: Negocio/0,1
f: Commerce/0,1
d: Handel/0,1
n: 買 "buy" + 賣 "sell" = 買賣, "business". 買 and 賣 differ only by tone, in every language here: Mandarin mǎi / mài, Cantonese maai5 / maai6. 賣 is 買 with 出 "out" on top: buying is taking in, selling is sending out.
nt: Stephen Li's dictionary: 買 mai33, 賣 mai32. Same tone split as Cantonese.

@ Word Fun | Brothers
g: younger brother / older brother
y: 兄/hing1/1 ; 弟/dai6/0
m: 弟/dì/0 ; 兄/xiong/1
t: 兄/hein33/1 ; 弟/ai32/0
s: Hermanos/0,1
f: Frères/0,1
d: Brüder/0,1
n: 弟 "younger brother" + 兄 "older brother" = 弟兄, "brothers", also "brothers in arms". Mandarin also says 兄弟 xiōngdì, older first. Cantonese only says 兄弟 hing1 dai6.
nt: Stephen Li's dictionary: 兄弟 hein33 ai32 "brothers". 弟 alone is hai21, but it changes to ai32 in 兄弟.
